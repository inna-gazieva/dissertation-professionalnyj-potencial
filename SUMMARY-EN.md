# Extended English Summary

This is an extended English summary of the Russian-language doctoral dissertation. The full text (five chapters and appendices with survey data) is published in this repository in Russian; the file map is given [at the end](#repository-map). In case of any discrepancy, the Russian text prevails.

## 1. Research problem

The dissertation addresses a contradiction at the core of Russian higher education: society needs a scientifically grounded approach to forming the professional potential of student youth — the condition of sustainable socio-professional reproduction and workforce sovereignty — yet no sufficient methodological framework for studying and shaping that potential exists.

The symptoms are well documented. According to federal statistical observation (VTR-2021), one in four university graduates in Russia does not work in the field of their degree. In a 2021 survey of 4,000 young professionals (Institute of Sociology, FCTAS RAS), 38% reported that the content of their work does not match their university specialization, and 24% would not choose the same field again. The competence-based approach that dominates higher education cannot resolve this on its own: the professional structure of society changes faster than curricula, universities respond slowly to labor-market signals, and employers cannot articulate a stable model of the graduate they need.

The dissertation argues that what remains stable amid these changes is the **value system** of the young person. Students who understand the values of their profession and their own life values accept new knowledge more readily, apply it more effectively, and remain adaptable — which makes values, rather than competences alone, the productive foundation for analyzing and forming professional potential.

## 2. Object, subject, and aim

- **Object:** the formation of the professional potential of youth in the higher education system.
- **Subject:** professional and life-meaning values of student youth as the basis for forming their professional potential.
- **Aim:** to develop the foundations of a value-based approach to the sociological diagnostics and formation of the professional potential of youth in higher education.

## 3. Hypotheses

- **Main hypothesis:** the effectiveness of forming and realizing the professional potential of youth in higher education is ensured not only by the conditions a university creates, but by the correspondence of those conditions to the values the students actually share. *(Confirmed.)*
- **Additional hypothesis 1:** students' involvement in each key area of extracurricular activity is determined by the values they share — each area of activity corresponds to specific values. *(Confirmed.)*
- **Additional hypothesis 2:** the values followed by student youth can be grouped by semantic social unity, and these value groups influence the formation of professional potential with different strength. *(Confirmed.)*

## 4. Key definitions

**Professional potential of youth in the higher education system** is defined as *the readiness of young people — determined by social values — to use their personal psychological attributes and the professional competences formed at university for purposeful, productive work activity.* Professional potential is thus understood as an integral characteristic of the formation of two groups of values: **life-meaning values** (which give life meaning and direction) and **professional values** (which set orientations in work and career).

**Formation of professional potential** is conceptualized as a social process: a set of social actions aimed at creating conditions for the interiorization of values and for cultivating students' readiness to develop and use their professional competences and personal qualities. The process has a defined structure — its constitutive states (the state of professional potential diagnosed at the beginning and end of each academic year), stages (entry into university; the main stage of study and upbringing, divided by academic years; graduation followed by employment or further study), duration (the entire period of study), and conditions (the character and quality of state and university youth policy).

## 5. Theoretical framework

The approach builds on three bodies of scholarship: (1) classical and contemporary treatments of "possibility" and "potential" and adjacent categories — social capital (Coleman), cultural capital (Bourdieu), human potential, labor potential, talent management; (2) theoretical and empirical value research — Rokeach; Inglehart and Welzel; Schwartz and Bilsky; and the Russian tradition (Yadov, Lapin, Zdravomyslov, Zubok and Chuprov); (3) research on formation as a social process (Zaslavskaya, Shtompka [Sztompka], Rezvik [Reznik], and others) and on university youth policy practices in Russia and abroad.

## 6. The empirical model

The author's empirical model treats each social value as a four-element structure, measured by four indicators:

1. **value attitude** (ценностное отношение) — the ideal attitude toward social reality within the given value;
2. **value preference** (ценностное предпочтение) — the significance assigned to each indicator describing the value;
3. **value disposition** (ценностная установка) — the readiness to act in accordance with the value;
4. **value action** (ценностное действие) — actual behavior following the value.

Comparing these levels reveals the gaps between declared significance and real action. The list of values examined is drawn from the values declared by the Russian state (traditional values and the normative value set proposed by the Ministry of Science and Higher Education as reference points for youth work) — that is, the model diagnoses how far the officially desired value image of students corresponds to their actual value structure.

## 7. Data

The main empirical base is the author's survey **"Traditional and Strategic Values of Student Youth" (17 May – 1 June 2023, N = 4,338**, full-time students of RANEPA, conducted within the Priority 2030 program). It is complemented by six further surveys led by the author in 2021–2024 (samples from N = 345 to N = 18,392, including a 2024 seven-university study, N = 3,864), and by secondary analysis of VCIOM, FOM, and European Social Survey (Round 9, Russia, N = 2,437) data. Line distributions and data tables are published in the [appendices](prilozheniya/) of this repository.

## 8. Key findings

**8.1. Integral indicator of professional potential.** The model aggregates the formation of two groups of values — terminal and instrumental — into a single integral indicator (scale 1 = highest to 5 = lowest formation). For the surveyed population the indicator equals **2.2** (terminal values 2.1, instrumental values 2.3): a fairly high level of formation, with terminal values formed somewhat better than instrumental ones.

**8.2. Value structure: core and reserves.** By degree of adherence, values distribute across structural levels: a **core** (fully shared), an upper, middle, and lower **reserve**, and a **periphery** (not shared). The core proved narrow — only three values: professional experience; development and self-realization; human rights and freedoms. The upper reserve contains eight values (moral values — humanism, justice, dignity, responsibility — and state-oriented values — patriotism, service to the Fatherland, continuity of generations, unity of the peoples of Russia). Instrumental values are absent from the core, indicating their clear undervaluation among students; no value fell into the periphery.

**8.3. Three-factor value space.** Factor analysis confirmed the model (all four indicators of almost every value loaded onto the same factor) and produced a three-factor space of professional potential: **values of professional self-determination**, **existential life-meaning values**, and **civil-society values**.

**8.4. Eight typological groups.** Cluster analysis in this three-factor space yielded eight typological groups of students, each named after a character of Russian classical literature whose traits match the group's factor coordinates: Pavel Korchagin, Yermolai Lopakhin, Andrei Prozorov, Sergei Golubkov, Grigory Pechorin, Alexander Chatsky, Alexei Vronsky, Sergei Talberg. For every group, "steering" values are identified — values that can serve as motivators when forming that group's professional potential, including via adjustment of the university's social environment.

**8.5. Differential strength of value influence.** The three factor groups influence the formation of professional potential with different strength: values of professional self-determination are the strongest, civil-society values intermediate, existential life-meaning values the weakest. Adherence to these values also predicts the role students take in university events (organizer, volunteer, participant, spectator): e.g., "Korchagin" and "Vronsky" types are active participants in nearly all events, "Lopakhin" and "Pechorin" types are essentially disengaged from university life.

**8.6. Practical pathway.** The most effective route of university youth personnel policy is the inclusion of students in practical activity during their studies — research and project work, and employment within the university itself — matched to the values each typological group actually shares.

## 9. Scientific novelty

The dissertation develops: (1) an original approach to professional potential as a sociological category grounded in values; (2) an original value-based methodology for studying professional potential as a social phenomenon; (3) an integral indicator of the formation of professional potential, built and tested on survey data; (4) an approach to value-based diagnostics of professional potential (three-factor space, typological groups, "steering" values); (5) a tested approach to analyzing the process of forming professional potential in universities.

## 10. Significance

**Theoretical:** a value-based (re)definition of professional potential; a four-element empirical model of value analysis applicable beyond this study; a three-factor typological model of the student value space. **Practical:** the toolkit can be embedded in university youth policy for value diagnostics of students, for designing evidence-based extracurricular programming per typological group, and in state youth (personnel) policy; an "HR-platform" software product registered by the author's team is already in use at RANEPA.
